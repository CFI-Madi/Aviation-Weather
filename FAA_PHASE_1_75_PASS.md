# FAA Phase 1.75 Pass

## Scope

This pass focused only on the remaining high-risk instructional areas left after Phase 1.5:

- engine icing
- fog decision-making
- PIREP nuance and reporting expectations
- advanced and edge weather products
- residual procedural or regulatory-sounding wording inside those topics

## What Was Corrected

### 1. Engine Icing

- Reframed `m7:s7_5` so the section now separates:
  - atmospheric setup that can support icing
  - aircraft-specific response that depends on design, systems, and POH/AFM procedures
- Removed wording that could be read as a universal aircraft-behavior claim.
- Reworked the carb-ice quick-reference tiles so they now read as training context rather than universal operational rules.
- Kept HIWC scoped as advanced weather awareness rather than a general GA engine rule.

### 2. Fog Decision-Making

- Reframed `m9:s9_4` as risk awareness and planning context rather than a prediction tool.
- Softened the fog-toolkit language so it now emphasizes signals, risk factors, and monitoring instead of forecasting certainty.
- Changed the sunrise/burn-off block from a “rule of thumb” toward a variability-focused trend note.
- Softened the `q_m9_4` answer and explanation so they no longer predict fog formation as a certainty.

### 3. PIREP Language

- Tightened `m13:s13_4` so it now emphasizes:
  - safety value
  - workload-permitting nuance
  - improved system awareness
- Avoided duty-style wording and avoided implying a blanket reporting requirement.
- Softened related quiz copy so prompt filing is encouraged when workload permits rather than presented as an unconditional obligation.

### 4. Advanced / Edge Weather Content

- Tightened `m19:s19_1` so space weather is framed more clearly as:
  - awareness for most GA pilots
  - more operationally significant for polar, oceanic, and high-latitude operations
- Tightened `m20:s20_4` so volcanic ash, tropical cyclones, and space weather are presented as specialized advisory topics rather than universal decision rules.
- Softened the volcanic-ash quiz explanation to emphasize severe damage risk without overstating one exact outcome as guaranteed.

## Validation Alignment

- `m7:engine-icing` remains `needs_review`
  - safer wording now, but still partially dependent on aircraft-specific procedure context
- `m9:s9_4` remains `needs_review`
  - the lesson is more uncertainty-aware, but still includes operational coaching
- `m13:s13_4` remains `needs_review`
  - the duty/reporting tone is safer, but stronger AIM-aligned review is still needed before marketing it as FAA-authoritative
- `m19:s19_1` remains `needs_review`
  - scoped better now, but still should not imply precise operational doctrine
- Added `m20:s20_4` as `training_simplification`
  - it is an awareness-oriented survey of specialized advisory products, not a full FAA procedural treatment

## What Is Now Safer to Present Publicly

- The app now more clearly distinguishes:
  - weather-risk awareness
  - practical study guidance
  - aircraft-specific action that must come from the POH/AFM
- Fog and PIREP lessons are less likely to be misread as legal or procedural mandates.
- Advanced hazard lessons now read as specialized awareness content instead of one-size-fits-all operating doctrine.

## What Still Should Not Be Marketed as FAA-Authoritative

- Engine-icing aircraft-response guidance beyond high-level weather awareness
- PIREP expectation language as if it were a universal FAA reporting duty
- Space-weather material as if it were equally operational for all GA users
- Study-flow or product-priority shortcuts as if they were official FAA checklists

## Remaining Unresolved Items

- A broader cleanup of legacy wording outside this pass may still be worthwhile before strong public trust claims.
- Remaining `needs_review` records still need a tighter source pass before any “fully FAA-backed” marketing language would be appropriate.
